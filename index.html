        The atlas heart is one still shape. Atrium bends it with a simplified motion: the atria squeeze first, then the ventricles squeeze inward, twist slightly and pull the valve plane down toward the apex, and the big arteries swell as blood rushes in.

        Timing follows typical adult values: about 0.3 seconds of squeezing at 75 beats a minute. As the rate rises, the resting part of each beat shortens much more than the squeeze. Real walls also thicken as they contract, which this model cannot show. The valve leaflets and their cords are hand-built and simplified; their timing follows the same beat.

        Blood is drawn as glowing points that shine through the walls. It moves only when the valves in its path are open, so it gathers behind a shut valve. Speeds are not to scale: a real trip around the whole body takes about a minute. The lungs and body are not modelled; they appear as simple loops.

        The ECG is a textbook-shaped trace generated from the beat's timing, not a recording; real ECGs differ from person to person and from lead to lead. The atria also recover electrically, but that small wave is hidden inside the QRS. The conduction system is drawn by Atrium, not taken from the atlas.

        The heart sounds are made by Atrium, not recorded. The first sound, lub, is mostly the mitral and tricuspid valves closing; the second, dub, the aortic and pulmonary valves. Each is really two valves a few hundredths of a second apart. In slow motion each sound keeps its real length, but that small gap is stretched so you can hear both valves. The sound comes from the valves, blood and walls vibrating as the valves snap shut, not from the leaflets slapping together.
